Abstract
Reading and authoring mathematical diagrams is difficult for Visually Impaired (VI) readers. We present PreFigure, a system for
creating mathematical diagrams from high-level descriptions that
compile to SVG and support interactive, layered exploration with
audio rendering and sonification via the diagcess library. Tactile
diagrams suitable for embossing can also be created from the same
descriptions. PreFigure can be used within document authoring
workflows (e.g., PreTeXt) and through the PreFigure Playground, a
standalone web-based editor whose input elements are designed to
be accessible.
In addition to describing this system, we report on Stage 1 of an
ongoing user study conducted at a secondary school for the blind.
Eighteen Grade 10 students (17 blind, 1 low vision) were evaluated on their comprehension of a set of diagrams either through
traditional tactile media or through the computer-based PreFigure
Playground with screen reader support. We also conducted semistructured discussions with geometry teachers. Observations and
participant feedback highlight recurring barriers in tactile materials (e.g., dense grids, labeling ambiguity, physical distortion, and
confusion between 2D and 3D representations). We identify actionable usability requirements for digital diagram interaction (e.g.,
clearer confirmation and error feedback, focus management, and
consistent conventions). The results suggest that computer-based
PreFigure representations are feasible for secondary-level learners
and can complement tactile methods for diagram comprehension.
